Basics of Nurse Licensure

Before placing a single nurse, every recruiter must understand how nursing licenses work at the foundational level.

🎓

What Is a Nursing License?

A state-issued legal authorization to practice nursing. Without it, practicing is illegal. Issued by each state's Board of Nursing (BON). Separate from passing the NCLEX exam.

MANDATORY
📋

The NCLEX Exam

All nurses pass NCLEX-RN (for RNs) or NCLEX-PN (for LPNs) once. National — the same everywhere. Passing NCLEX ≠ having a license. The state must still issue it.

NATIONAL EXAM
🏛

State Boards of Nursing

Each of the 50 states (plus DC) has its own BON that issues licenses, sets CE requirements, and handles disciplinary matters. 51 separate authorities total.

51 BOARDS
🔄

Renewal Requirements

Licenses renew every 2 years. Requires continuing education hours (CEUs), updated info, and fees. Always verify license is active, not just that it exists.

EVERY 2 YEARS
🔍

License Verification

Use Nursys.com — the national nurse licensure database — to check active licenses, expiration dates, and disciplinary history across participating states.

NURSYS.COM
⚠

License Status Types

A license can be: Active, Expired, Suspended, Revoked, or Restricted. Always check status, not just existence. Travelers often let licenses lapse between assignments.

VERIFY STATUS
💡 Recruiter Rule #1: A nurse can hold multiple licenses simultaneously — one compact multistate license plus individual licenses in non-compact states. Always verify independently on Nursys or the state BON before placing.
  • RN: 2–4 year degree, NCLEX-RN. Most travel positions. Covered under NLC compact.
  • LPN/LVN: 1-year diploma, NCLEX-PN. Also covered under NLC compact since 2018.
  • APRN (NPs, CRNAs, CNMs, CNSs): Master's+ level. NOT covered by the NLC compact. Must obtain state authorization in each state separately.
  • CNA: State-certified (not licensed). Registry requirements vary by state.
  • Current status: Active, Inactive, Expired, Suspended, Revoked
  • Expiration date: Critical — many nurses let licenses lapse between assignments
  • Compact privileges: Whether multistate privileges are currently active
  • Disciplinary actions: Public orders, restrictions, conditions on practice

Not all states participate in Nursys. For non-participating states, go directly to the state BON website.

  • "What state do you currently call home?"
  • "Is your nursing license currently active and in good standing?"
  • "Do you hold licenses in any other states?"
  • "Have you ever had any action taken against your license?"
  • "Are you open to pursuing endorsements in other states?"

Compact vs. Non-Compact License

The Nurse Licensure Compact (NLC) is the single most important concept in travel nursing placement. Understand it cold.

🔵

Compact State License (NLC)

A nurse whose Primary State of Residence (PSOR) is a compact state holds a multistate license. This single license grants practice privileges in all 41 compact states — no extra applications needed.

41 STATES
🔴

Non-Compact State License

A nurse residing in a non-compact state holds a single-state license. Valid only in the issuing state. To work anywhere else, they must apply for and receive a separate license in each target state.

9 STATES + DC
🔑 Critical: RESIDENCE vs. LICENSURE. Compact privilege is tied to where the nurse lives (PSOR), not where they first got licensed. A nurse licensed in Texas but now living in California is subject to California's non-compact rules.

When a nurse permanently moves, they must notify their home state BON within 30 days. Moving to compact = gains privileges. Moving to non-compact = loses them.

  • Fast (2–4 weeks): Most compact-to-compact transfers
  • Moderate (4–8 weeks): Most non-compact states with standard applications
  • Slow (8–16+ weeks): California (especially international grads), Pennsylvania, Massachusetts

No re-taking NCLEX. Some agencies pay endorsement fees as a recruitment benefit.

Original NLC: 2000. Enhanced NLC (eNLC) with 11 uniform requirements including background checks: 2018. New York joined September 2023 — a major Northeast breakthrough. States join through legislation. Non-compact states remain so due to union opposition or legislative gridlock.

Nurse Moves TX to CA Permanently

PSOR Change — Compact to Non-Compact
1
Before the Move

Nurse holds TX multistate license. Can legally practice in all 41 compact states without additional licenses.

2
Move Established

Nurse gets CA driver's license, registers to vote, files taxes in CA. CA becomes legal PSOR. Must notify TX BON within 30 days.

3
Compact Privileges Lost

Cannot legally use TX multistate license anywhere once CA residency is established. Must apply for CA endorsement immediately. CA can take 4 to 6 months.

4
Recruiter Strategy

Help candidate apply for CA license immediately. Offer nearby compact assignments (NV, AZ, OR, WA) while CA processes. Never place in an unlicensed state.

⚠ The nurse cannot use their TX multistate license anywhere once CA is their PSOR, until the CA license is issued.

Travel Nurse on 13-Week Assignment

Temporary Assignment — No PSOR Change
1
Setup

Nurse lives in FL (compact), holds FL multistate license. Takes 13-week assignment in TN (also compact).

2
PSOR Stays in FL

A temporary work assignment does NOT change PSOR. They keep FL home, mailing address, driver's license, and voter registration in FL.

3
No Additional License Needed

FL multistate license valid in TN. Travel nurses can take back-to-back compact assignments for years while maintaining their compact-state PSOR.

✓ Best case — compact nurse, compact assignment. Zero licensing delays. Place immediately.

Military Spouse — PCS Orders

Military Exception to PSOR Rules
1
Setup

Military nurse stationed at Fort Hood TX. Official home of record: Virginia (compact). Physically living in TX on military orders.

2
PSOR Exception

Federal law (SCRA + Military Spouses Residency Relief Act) allows military members and spouses to maintain home-state PSOR regardless of station. Virginia remains PSOR.

3
VA License Valid in TX

VA multistate license covers TX. Documentation needed: military orders plus VA driver's license or PSOR affidavit.

🛈 Always ask military nurses for their official home of record — it determines PSOR, not their current mailing address.

Compact Nurse Wants Non-Compact State

Endorsement Required — Plan Ahead
1
Setup

Nurse lives in NC (compact). Wants 13-week assignment at UPMC Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania (non-compact).

2
Compact Does Not Cover PA

NC multistate privileges do NOT extend to PA. A separate PA license is required regardless of compact status.

3
Endorsement Process

Apply to PA State Board of Nursing: application fee (~$100), license verification, background check. Processing: 6 to 10 weeks.

4
Recruiter Strategy

Initiate PA endorsement 8 to 10 weeks before start date. Check if PA offers Temporary Practice Permits (TPPs) to allow work while full license processes.

⚠ Every non-compact state requires its own license regardless of compact status. Plan 8+ weeks for every non-compact placement.

How State Taxes Are Different

Tax implications are a key part of total compensation. Recruiters who understand taxes build better candidate trust.

💚

No State Income Tax

Top travel destinations where nurses keep more take-home pay:

  • Texas (TX)
  • Florida (FL)
  • Nevada (NV)
  • Washington (WA)
  • Wyoming (WY)
  • South Dakota (SD)
  • Alaska (AK)
  • Tennessee (TN) — wages exempt
  • New Hampshire (NH) — wages exempt
MAX TAKE-HOME
🟠

High State Income Tax

Critical context for pay discussions:

  • California (CA) — up to 13.3%
  • Hawaii (HI) — up to 11%
  • New Jersey (NJ) — up to 10.75%
  • Oregon (OR) — up to 9.9%
  • Minnesota (MN) — up to 9.85%
  • Massachusetts (MA) — flat 5%
  • Illinois (IL) — flat 4.95%
AFFECTS NET PAY
🏠

Tax Home Concept

A nurse's tax home is their PSOR. To receive tax-free stipends (housing, meals) a nurse must maintain a permanent residence in their home state, incur real expenses there, and duplicate those expenses at the assignment.

Nurses who abandon their tax home lose stipend eligibility. Always refer to a travel nurse tax specialist.

The Driver's License Connection

The clearest way to explain nursing licensure to candidates, clients, and colleagues.

🚗 The Core Analogy: A nursing license works like a driver's license — earned once through a test, issued by your home state, must be renewed, and subject to state rules. The compact is like an interstate highway system where certain states recognize each other's licenses, letting you practice without stopping for a new license at every border.

📞 Use This Script with Candidates: "Think of your nursing license like your driver's license. You passed the test once — the NCLEX. Your home state issued it. As long as your home state is part of the Compact, you can practice in 40 other states without doing anything extra. But just like you would need a new driver's license if you permanently moved states, your nursing license follows the same rule."
